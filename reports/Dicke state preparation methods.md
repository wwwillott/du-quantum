# Gate counts, not asymptotics, decide Dicke preparation

Nearly every exact method for preparing a Dicke state D(N,k) on gate-based hardware comes from one identity: a Dicke state splits on its last qubit into two smaller Dicke states. Bärtschi and Eidenbenz turned that identity into a circuit with O(kN) gates and O(N) depth that runs on a plain 1D chain with no ancillas and no measurements. The literature since then has gone in three directions. The first cuts the constant factor: Mukherjee et al. reach 5Nk−5k²−2N CNOTs, and Aktar et al.'s divide-and-conquer reaches 5k(N−k)−3(N+k)+5. The second cuts depth: O(k log(N/k)) on all-to-all hardware, O(√(Nk)) on grids, and O(k log(N/k)+n₂) on an n₁×n₂ grid with a matching Ω(n₂) lower bound. The third uses brute force for tiny instances: exact CNOT search gives D(4,2) in 6 CNOTs and D(6,3) in 25, and a hand-tuned grid circuit gives D(8,2) in 31 CZ on a 2×4 patch. Methods that use mid-circuit measurement and feedforward promise constant depth, but almost all of them are approximate or post-selected. The one exact, deterministic family (LAQCC) needs hundreds of ancillas at hackathon sizes. Analog and native-interaction schemes (STIRAP, blockade, global Ising control, cavity QED) are approximate by design. Every hardware result tells the same story. Measured fidelity falls by about 1.5% per compiled two-qubit gate. The best CZ-only D(8,2) reached F = 0.631, and no Google Sycamore or Willow Dicke experiment has been published. For a team building exact D(N,n) on Willow, the published targets are D(4,2) ≤ 6, D(6,3) ≤ 23–25, D(8,2) ≤ 27–31, D(8,4) ≤ 49 and D(10,5) ≤ 85 entangling gates. Their current pipeline beats the literature only at D(4,2). The one gap nobody has published on is building the circuit directly on a defective, error-weighted hardware patch and relabelling qubits instead of routing.

## One splitting identity underlies almost every exact circuit

### The Bärtschi–Eidenbenz recursion and its descendants (all-to-all and 1D chains)

The base idea is simple. Write the Dicke state as "the last qubit is 1 with probability k/N, and then the rest is D(N−1,k−1); otherwise the last qubit is 0 and the rest is D(N−1,k)." Concretely, |D^n_l⟩ = √(l/n)·|D^(n−1)_(l−1)⟩|1⟩ + √((n−l)/n)·|D^(n−1)_l⟩|0⟩ ([B&E 2019](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.07358v1)). **Bärtschi & Eidenbenz 2019** (FCT 2019, arXiv:1904.07358) start from the classical string |0^(N−k)1^k⟩ and apply N−1 "split and cyclic shift" (SCS) blocks. Each block decides the last qubit with exactly the right weight and slides the block of 1s one position left in the branch where a 1 moved out. Each SCS block is one 2-qubit controlled rotation followed by a staircase of k−1 three-qubit doubly-controlled rotations, all on neighbouring wires except the moving end qubit. The construction is exact, uses no ancillas, needs **at most 5kn+O(n) CNOTs**, and reaches **O(n) depth** because successive staircases can be "pushed into each other." All of this also holds on a linear nearest-neighbour chain, where the end qubit is sifted up and down with SWAPs in blocks of about k ([B&E 2019](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.07358v1)). The exact count for the unoptimised circuit is 5(nk − k(k+1)/2 − n + 1) + 4(n−1) CNOTs ([Mukherjee et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01681)). The same unitary prepares every D(n,l) with l ≤ k from the matching unary input. It therefore extends to arbitrary symmetric states with O(n²) gates and, run in reverse, compresses symmetric states into ⌈log(n+1)⌉ qubits ([B&E 2019](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.07358v1)).

**Cruz et al. 2019** (arXiv:1807.05572) solved the W state (k=1) separately. Their building block B(p) splits one excitation between two qubits with weight p. A chain of B(1/N), B(1/(N−1)), …, B(1/2) gives a nearest-neighbour W state in N−1 steps. A binary "dichotomy tree" of B(1/2)-type splits gives log₂N depth, provided the root qubit can reach about log₂N partners ([Cruz et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.05572)). The practical count is **2N−3 CNOTs** ([Aktar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.12435)). The AshN paper reports a provable lower bound of (15N−3)/14 CNOTs for W_N and numerical evidence that 2N−3 is the true minimum ([arXiv:2502.03612](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03612v1)).

**Mukherjee, Maitra, Gaurav & Roy 2020** (IEEE TQE; arXiv:2007.01681) observed that each B&E gadget only ever sees a few input patterns. Any unitary that agrees on those patterns will do, so they substituted cheaper ones. They used a 2-qubit piece with 1 CNOT plus single-qubit gates, deleted pieces that act as identity on every reachable state, and collapsed 3-qubit gates whose control is provably |1⟩. The result is **5Nk−5k²−2N CNOTs** for k>1 and 2N−2 for W, which brings D(8,2) from 58 to 44 and D(4,2) from 22 to 12 ([Mukherjee et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01681)). Aktar et al. later noted that these reductions need either more than LNN connectivity or large constant overheads ([Aktar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.12435)). This "partially defined unitary" trick is the most reusable idea in the whole field.

**Aktar, Bärtschi, Badawy & Eidenbenz 2022** (IEEE TQE; arXiv:2112.12435) introduced divide-and-conquer. First "divide" the k excitations between two halves: write the superposition over (k₁, k−k₁) with weights C(N/2,k₁)·C(N/2,k−k₁) in unary on one half, using controlled rotations. A rotation whose target is still a classical 0/1 costs only 1 CNOT. Then subtract k₁ from the other half's unary register, and "conquer" both halves in parallel with mirror-image B&E unitaries. On LNN this gives D(4,2) in **10 CNOTs at depth 11**, against the previous 12 at depth 21, and D(6,3) in 32–33 CNOTs ([Aktar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.12435)). Their follow-up on Quantinuum hardware tightens the Hamming-weight bounds on the base unitary and reaches **5K(N−K)−3(N+K)+5 CNOTs** at O(N) depth: 7 for D(4,2), 23 for D(6,3), 49 for D(8,4) and 85 for D(10,5). That paper, however, describes the 2K−1-CNOT divide step as "on an all-to-all connectivity" ([arXiv:2210.03048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03048)). The TQE paper's "ladder" column lists 7 and 23 for a 2×(N/2) ladder, but its PDF layout is garbled ([Aktar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.12435)). Whether these counts hold on a ladder (a subgraph of Willow) or need all-to-all connectivity is unresolved until someone builds D(6,3) in 23 CZ on a ladder.

**Bärtschi & Eidenbenz 2022** (IEEE QCE; arXiv:2207.09998) generalise SCS into a "weight distribution block" (WDB). A WDB takes "l excitations on n qubits" and splits it into the correctly weighted superposition of "i on m qubits, l−i on the rest," with amplitudes √(C(m,i)C(n−m,l−i)/C(n,l)). It acts only on the last k qubits of each part. It converts unary to one-hot, adds a weighted superposition with a controlled-rotation stair, converts back, and subtracts with a Fredkin stair, at **O(k) depth and O(k²) gates** ([B&E 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09998)). SCS is the special case WDB^(n,1), and Cruz's log-depth W tree is the k=1 case. Arranging WDBs as a balanced tree gives **O(k log(N/k)) depth and O(kN) gates on all-to-all hardware, with no ancillas**. The paper's hand-tuned D(8,2) uses **27 CNOTs all-to-all** ([B&E 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09998)).

**Yuan & Zhang 2025** (arXiv:2505.15413) reduce all-to-all depth to **O(log k · log(N/k) + k)**. They borrow qubits that sit idle at a given tree level as scratch space, which speeds up the unary/one-hot conversions and Toffoli parts. There are no external ancillas and no measurements, and the scheme falls back to the O(N) chain when k = Θ(N). They report depth only, not gate counts ([Yuan & Zhang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.15413)).

**Wang, Tan, Cong & De Micheli 2024** (DATE 2024; arXiv:2401.01009) dropped hand design altogether. They run a shortest-path search backwards from the target state to |0…0⟩, where nodes are states, edges are gates with CNOT costs, and states equal up to single-qubit gates or qubit relabelling are merged. For 4-qubit uniform states this merging shrinks 8008 raw states to 47. The search is exact and assumes all-to-all connectivity, and it beats hand design: **D(4,2) in 6 CNOTs (vs 12), D(5,2) 16, D(6,2) 22, D(6,3) 25 (vs 33)** ([Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.01009)). Two caveats apply. The circuits are irregular and do not generalise. They are also optimal only within the {Ry, CNOT, CRy, MCRy} library: the same table gives W₄ = 7 and W₅ = 10, worse than the known 2N−3 = 5 and 7 ([Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.01009)).

Several later results restate the problem without beating these counts on qubits. Raveh & Nepomechie write the Dicke state as a matrix product state and turn it into a sequential circuit related to B&E's recursion ([PRA 110, 052438](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevA.110.052438)). Nepomechie and co-authors extend B&E to qudits and spin-s ([arXiv:2301.04989](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.04989); [arXiv:2402.03233](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.03233)). **Joshi & Vasconcelos 2026** (arXiv:2601.10693) reach constant depth only with unbounded fan-in CZ gates (QAC⁰) plus polynomially many ancillas, aimed at ion and neutral-atom global gates. They note that ordinary 2-qubit-gate circuits cannot beat logarithmic depth ([arXiv:2601.10693](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10693)). The older generic routes are superseded for exact Dicke states. Childs et al. 2000 is probabilistic. Kaye–Mosca 2004 and the Bacon–Harrow–Chuang Schur transform are ε-approximate. Plesch–Bužek 2009 has quadratic depth ([B&E 2022, Table I](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09998)).

### On a chain, the depth floor is already met

Light-cone arguments settle the 1D question. Every pair of qubits in a Dicke state is entangled, so their backward light cones must overlap. That forces **Ω(N) depth on a path, Ω(log N) all-to-all, and Ω(n₂) on an n₁×n₂ grid** for any measurement-free 2-qubit-gate circuit ([Yuan & Zhang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.15413)). B&E 2019's O(N) chain circuit is therefore depth-optimal up to constants, and the only remaining question on a chain is the constant factor. No paper gives a Dicke-specific lower bound on CNOT count beyond connectivity arguments and the small exact searches. Mukherjee proves only per-gadget minimality ([Mukherjee et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.01681)), and the field has no closed form below 5K(N−K)−3(N+K)+5 for chains or ladders.

## On a square grid, depth floors at the patch's long side

### Grid constructions: tiles, snakes and balanced halving

**B&E 2022's grid construction** cuts the grid into tiles of k qubits, each threaded by a short snake (a 1D path) whose ends touch the snakes of neighbouring tiles. WDBs first pass excitation weight down the leftmost column of tiles. Then they pass it along every row in parallel. Finally small 1D Dicke unitaries run inside each tile. There are about √(N/k) sequential O(k)-depth WDB steps per direction, giving **depth O(√(Nk)) and O(kN) gates with no ancillas** on grids of size Ω(√(N/s)) × O(√(Ns)) ([B&E 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.09998)). The full proof uses one long snake per row instead of full tiles, because idle qubits inside partly filled tiles block interactions. Injecting the weight in a central tile instead of a corner cuts depth by about 4×. On Θ(√(N/k)) × Θ(√(Nk)) grids the depth matches the graph diameter, so the authors conjecture it is optimal ([B&E 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.09998)). The paper's concrete grid circuit is the most useful number for Willow: **D(8,2) in 31 CNOTs on a grid**, versus an extrapolated 35 for Aktar's scheme. It absorbs a wire reversal by orienting two of the four leaf blocks the opposite way instead of using a SWAP network, which is permutation-symmetry relabelling in practice ([B&E 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.09998)). The authors call these WDB implementations "hand-tuned non-scalable." They also say their scalable WDBs, with Fredkin stairs, are not resource-efficient.

**Yuan & Zhang 2025** replace "column first, then rows" with balanced halving. They split the region into left and right halves, run a divide unitary on the two blocks straddling the midpoint, move one block into place, and recurse on both halves in parallel. The divides add up to O(k log(N/k)), and the block moves form a geometric series summing to O(n₂). The result is **depth O(k log(N/k) + n₂) when k ≥ n₂/n₁, and O(n₂) otherwise**, which is asymptotically optimal when k = O(n₂/log n₁) ([Yuan & Zhang](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15413)). In plain words, the depth floor on a grid is the long side of the patch, not N, which favours compact near-square patches. The paper gives no constants or gate counts, so it cannot say whether balanced halving beats B&E 2022 at N ≤ 20. The block-move step costs SWAPs that a Dicke circuit can often avoid by relabelling, since final qubit order is irrelevant. The paper does not exploit this.

### The only CZ-only grid experiment pays heavily for dead couplers

**Wang et al. 2026** (arXiv:2608.24084) ran D(8,2) on a 2×4 patch of a 105-qubit, 182-coupler chip with "the same hardware architecture as Zuchongzhi 3.0". The baseline was the 31-CZ B&E 2022 grid circuit, recompiled with the Mirror-SABRE router for each lattice variant, including ones with dead couplers. Their contribution is AshN control, which makes many native two-qubit gates available per coupler (CZ, iSWAP, √iSWAP, SWAP, a B-like gate), so logical gates and routing SWAPs merge into one pulse. **AshN needed 23 entangling gates and reached F = 0.736. The CZ version needed 31 and reached F = 0.631** ([Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24084)). The defect study carries the lesson for a CZ-only team. CZ fidelity "deteriorates rapidly" as couplers are removed, and the CZ circuit never certified genuine multipartite entanglement: its witness went from +0.059 on the perfect patch to +0.195 with three defects. AshN lost only about 0.04 in fidelity with three defective couplers ([Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24084)). Across benchmarks, CZ compilation to constrained topologies costs 1.65× more gates and 1.86× more depth than all-to-all, against 1.13× and 1.24× for AshN ([Wang et al. SM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.24084)).

### Heavy-hex and other sparse graphs have no native Dicke construction

IBM-hardware papers do not build for heavy-hex at all. Aktar et al. ran Dicke states with n ≤ 6 on IBM Sydney and Montreal using LNN circuits, explicitly because heavy-hex is sparse ([Aktar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.12435)). Mukherjee et al. analysed the "CNOT map" (interaction graph) of their circuit to fit IBM coupling maps ([Mukherjee et al.](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2007.01681)). B&E 2022 call heavy-hex "grid-like," meaning a grid maps onto it with at most constant-factor overhead, but give no explicit embedding ([B&E 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.09998)). The heavy-hex W-state work that exists uses mid-circuit feedforward on up to 104 qubits of ibm_marrakesh ([arXiv:2505.03111](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03111)) or analog dynamics ([arXiv:2605.18962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18962)). Collision-model Dicke circuits on IQM's square-lattice Emerald chip show what generic routing costs: a median of **25% extra two-qubit gates** (90th percentile 56.7%) across 150 transpiled circuits ([Vu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30176v1)). Noise-aware placement for this problem has so far been done only for GHZ states, using error-weighted tree selection on heavy-hex devices with up to 414 qubits ([arXiv:2312.15170](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.15170)). It has not been done for Dicke states.

## Measurement buys constant depth but almost never determinism

The adaptive literature sorts into three groups: exact and deterministic, exact only on a heralded outcome (post-selected), and approximate. Only the first group can produce a circuit that is "exact when noiseless" on every branch.

**Buhrman, Folkertsma, Loff, Neumann et al. (LAQCC)**, Quantum 8, 1552 (2024), arXiv:2307.14840, define a model of constant-depth nearest-neighbour grid circuits. Intermediate measurements feed a log-depth classical computation that controls later gates ([arXiv:2307.14840](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14840)). The W state is built by "uncompress then compress." First, put a log N-qubit index register into an exact uniform superposition over N values. Second, fan the index bits out next to every system qubit and flip qubit i when the index equals i. Third, erase the index using the now one-hot system register, with Hadamards, fan-out and CZ. The result is **exact, deterministic, O(1) depth, and O(N log N) qubits on an N × log N grid**. Their Dicke construction for k = O(√N) has four steps. It fills k index registers. It filters out "birthday collisions," where two indices pick the same qubit, using exact amplitude amplification. It measures pairwise comparators to fix an ordering. Finally it cleans up. This costs **O(N² log N) qubits**. Arbitrary k takes O(log N) depth via a factoradic-to-combinatorial-number conversion ([arXiv:2307.14840v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.14840v5)). The constructions are asymptotic. The paper's own N=4, k=2 filling example uses 24 qubits, and its lemma statements give inconsistent qubit counts. Luo–Chen–Ma also point out that LAQCC under-counts the width of exact Hamming-weight gates ([arXiv:2608.01144](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01144v1)).

**Luo, Chen & Ma 2026** (arXiv:2608.01144) make a "uniform subset superposition," a superposition over all k-subsets stored as k sorted binary indices, in constant adaptive depth. They prepare k independent uniform index registers and measure all pairwise "greater-than" bits, which reveals the ranking. Classically chosen offsets then turn that ranking into a strictly increasing list. They write 1s at the listed positions and erase the indices. Success means the measured "descent number" hit the planned value, which has probability **≥ 1/k**. The result is exact when it succeeds, with O(N² + k² log² N + kN log N log log N) ancillas, assuming all-to-all connectivity ([arXiv:2608.01144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01144)). Running R ≈ c·k copies in parallel drives failure below e^(−c) at the same depth, but the scheme is still not deterministic ([arXiv:2608.01144v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01144v1)).

**Bäumer & Woerner 2025** (arXiv:2408.03064, Phys. Rev. Research 7, 023120) is the fan-out paper. A correction to a common citation error: **arXiv:2406.07611 is not the fan-out paper**. That arXiv number is Koh, Koh & Thompson's readout-error mitigation for mid-circuit measurements and feedforward (PRX Quantum 7, 010317). It gives up to about 60% error reduction on resets, GHZ states and teleportation, and prepares no Dicke or W states ([arXiv:2406.07611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07611)). Bäumer & Woerner put system qubits and ancillas alternately on a 1D line. They realise **fan-out (1 control, n targets) with about 3n−1 CNOTs in 5 CNOT layers, one measurement round and one feedforward round**, against 2n−1 layers for the unitary fan-out. A long-range CNOT takes 7 layers and 4n−2 CNOTs, against about 2n layers unitarily ([arXiv:2408.03064](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.03064)). On ibm_kyiv, with feedforward emulated in post-processing, the measurement-based version beat the unitary one from about 7 system qubits for fan-out and about 9 for long-range CNOT. The paper's conclusion says 6 and 8 instead, so it contradicts itself ([arXiv:2408.03064](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.03064)). This is the realistic building block for grid hardware, because it needs only a line.

**Piroli, Styliaris & Cirac 2024** (PRL 133, 230401; arXiv:2403.07604) relax exactness. Prepare each qubit in √(1−p)|0⟩+√p|1⟩ with p = k/N. Measure the excitation number modulo 2^ℓ with an ancilla register and an inverse QFT. Keep the state if the result equals k, and retry otherwise. Infidelity falls as about e^(−2^ℓ), and success probability is **≥ 1/√(8πk)**. Measuring the full weight (about log₂N bits) makes the kept state exact. Amplitude amplification removes the retries, but at depth O(k^(1/4) ℓ²) ([arXiv:2403.07604v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07604v3)). Their 2021 W protocol is exact but needs sequential LOCC rounds, so it takes O(N) time ([arXiv:2403.07604v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07604v3)). **Smith, Pokharel, Maurya & Takita (IBM) 2026** (arXiv:2609.08925) apply the same "binomial product, then project" pattern to hardware. They project with one "distribute–collapse" round for W states, and for Dicke states they phase-estimate the weight bit by bit. Error-detection checks flag bad shots. W states for N = 5, 10, 15, 20 reached **F = 0.87, 0.79, 0.69, 0.59** with 86% down to 46% of shots kept. The protocol is approximate and post-selected ([arXiv:2609.08925](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08925)). Their timing model also puts measurement plus feedforward at about 24 two-qubit-gate durations, so "constant depth" understates wall-clock cost ([arXiv:2609.08925](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08925)).

Two more families measure the collective state. **Yu, Muleady, Wang, Schine, Gorshkov & Childs** (arXiv:2411.03428, PRL) prepare D(N,N/2) by rotating all qubits globally, measuring J_z (the Hamming weight), resetting if the result is too far off, and repeating until the weight is exactly N/2. That takes **O(log N) expected rounds** with polylog depth and ancillas. It is exact on termination but has no fixed round bound ([arXiv:2411.03428](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03428)). **Liu, Childs & Gottesman** (arXiv:2411.04019) symmetrise a sorted list into an equal superposition of its permutations. This gives exact Dicke states of any weight in Õ(log N) depth with O(N log N) ancillas ([arXiv:2411.04019](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04019)).

For the projection route the arithmetic is unforgiving at bonus sizes. Projecting the binomial product state succeeds with probability C(N,k)p^k(1−p)^(N−k). That is about 0.20 for D(15,7) and about 0.38 for a 15-qubit W state (computed from the bound and mechanism in [PSC24](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07604v3)). A Dicke state also cannot be repaired after a wrong-weight outcome with Pauli corrections. Changing the weight of a symmetric state needs a non-Pauli, permutation-symmetric operation, which is as hard as the original problem.

## Numerical search finds exact small circuits; analog methods only approximate

### Variational and numerically synthesised circuits

The useful numerical work here is exact search, not variational training. Wang et al.'s shortest-path synthesis (above) remains the best published source of small-case counts. Its geometric-mean CNOT count over the benchmark set is 10.9 against 13.0 for the best manual design, a 17% gain ([Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.01009)). **Gard et al. 2020** (npj Quantum Information 6, 10) contribute a design principle rather than a count. They start from a basis state with the right particle number and apply only 2-qubit "exchange" gates that act inside span{|01⟩,|10⟩}. The Hamming weight is then preserved exactly by construction, with a minimal number of parameters and nearest-neighbour coupling only ([APS 2020 abstract](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/mar/2020/f07/13)). Portfolio-VQE ansätze for tunable Dicke-like states are hand-built controlled-Ry staircases ([arXiv:2403.04296](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.04296)). Reinforcement-learning circuit discovery has been demonstrated only for generic states, with no Dicke-specific result ([arXiv:2601.18953](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18953v1); [arXiv:2511.08096](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08096)). The collision-model dataset of Vu et al. is a numerically optimised, finite-depth and therefore approximate protocol. It uses shuttle qubits and partial-SWAP collisions with tuned angles, and records the best noiseless fidelity found against depth for 5–14 qubits ([arXiv:2608.30176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30176)). Constrained-QAOA papers consume Dicke states as starting states for Hamming-weight-preserving XY mixers on up to 32 trapped-ion qubits. They add no new preparation circuit and name preparation overhead as the bottleneck ([He et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03857); [Niroula et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06290); [arXiv:2211.13080](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.13080)).

Excitation-preserving gates explain why some circuits keep the Hamming weight better than others. Gates in the XY/iSWAP/Givens family never change the number of 1s, so coherent over-rotation only reshuffles amplitude inside the correct weight shell. Rigetti calibrated the whole XY(θ) family from a single pulse ([arXiv:1912.04424](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.04424)). **AshN** (arXiv:2502.03612; Nature Physics 2025) reaches any SU(4) gate in one pulse. It made **W₁₀ with 9 two-qubit gates at F = 0.913 and D(4,2) with 8 SU(4) gates at F = 0.926**, with 99.37% average two-qubit fidelity ([arXiv:2502.03612](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03612v1)). The same paper states that N−1 two-qubit gates is the floor for W_N without ancillas. On a CZ-only chip, a real Givens rotation has two nonzero KAK coordinates and costs at least 2 CZ. CZ-compiled Dicke circuits therefore pay roughly twice the entangling count of XY-native ones: W₁₀ takes 9 AshN gates against 2N−3 = 17 CZ. That gap is a property of the gate set, not of the circuit design.

### Analog and native-interaction approaches

Analog methods spread excitations with engineered collective dynamics instead of discrete gates. All of them are approximate, and high-fidelity results exist only at small N. In trapped ions, **Hume, Chou, Rosenband & Wineland 2009** put the chain's shared motion into an m-phonon Fock state. One global pulse then converts each phonon into a spin flip spread symmetrically over all ions; they demonstrated this on 2 ions ([arXiv:0909.0046](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0909.0046)). Collective STIRAP, a delayed pair of global laser pulses, creates Dicke states of any size and tolerates moderate parameter errors ([arXiv:0802.3538](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0802.3538)), but it needs a phonon Fock state to start from ([arXiv:1208.3411](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1208.3411)). Global Mølmer–Sørensen interactions supply the many-qubit fan-out that constant-depth unitary schemes assume ([arXiv:2601.10693](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10693)). For all-to-all Ising (ZZ) coupling, **Stojanović & Nauth 2023** use three global pulses and two free-evolution periods to take |000⟩ to D(3,2) inside the symmetric subspace, and show D(4,2) ([arXiv:2302.12483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12483)). Muratori et al. extend optimal global control to nearest-neighbour Ising rings using the ring's dihedral symmetry ([arXiv:2512.19545](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.19545)).

For neutral atoms, chirped adiabatic passage under Rydberg blockade creates single collective excitations ([arXiv:2101.01987](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.01987)). Collective Rabi flopping in 3–16-atom ensembles gave Fock-like collective states with 62% and 48% fidelity ([PRL 112, 043602](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.112.043602)). An odd-length antiferromagnetic ring hosts a single delocalised defect, which is a W state, and gave **F ≈ 0.77 on 11 Rydberg atoms** ([arXiv:2510.17974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17974)). For superconducting qubits, simultaneous engineered couplings spread one excitation across a lattice in a single step, in time set by the largest lattice side. That gave **6-qubit W at 83.9% in 99 ns on a 3×2 lattice** and 7-qubit W at 79.6% ([arXiv:2605.18962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18962)). Cavity-QED adiabatic interpolation can select any |J,m⟩ Dicke state in theory ([arXiv:2608.22168](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22168)). Spinor BECs make twin-Fock states of about 11,000 atoms, but these are collective and not individually addressable ([arXiv:1702.03120](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.03120)). None of these are options on a CZ + PhasedXZ chip. Their main lesson is the time scaling: analog W spreading takes time set by the lattice side, which matches the Ω(n₂) light-cone floor for gate circuits.

Taking the effective per-gate fidelity F^(1/#2q) from these numbers gives about **0.985 for Wang's CZ circuit, 0.986 for IBM D(4,2), 0.984 for IQM D(10,5) and 0.991 for the H1-2 bound**. That is about 1.5% lost per two-qubit gate, three to five times the quoted per-gate error. The difference points to idling decoherence and readout as large hidden costs. Wang's chip had T2-Ramsey of only 5–21 µs and readout errors of 0.9–2.2% for |1⟩ against 0.5–1.9% for |0⟩ ([Wang et al. SM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.24084)). The IQM data show the failure mode directly. P_K, the probability of measuring the correct Hamming weight, fell from 0.82 at 21 gates to 0.23 at 201 gates, while fidelity conditioned on the right weight stayed much higher. For the same D(10,5) target, 84 versus 201 gates meant F = 0.26 versus F ≈ 0 ([Vu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30176v1)). On IBM, fidelity fell "largely linear[ly]" with CNOT count. Noise-adaptive compilation helped only when it did not drastically increase CNOTs, and readout mitigation added about +0.1 absolute fidelity ([Aktar et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.12435)). Certification has shifted from full tomography (3^N settings) to symmetry-based estimators. These include Somma-type lower bounds from three global settings ([arXiv:2210.03048](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.03048)), permutation-orbit direct fidelity estimation ([Vu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30176v1)), and collective-spin witnesses ([Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.24084)). No experiment reports dynamical decoupling or Hamming-weight post-selection as mitigation for Dicke states. **No Dicke experiment on Google Sycamore or Willow, and no hardware Dicke state with k ≥ 2 built using feedforward, appears in the literature.**

These gaps are worth closing. At an observed ~1.5% loss per gate, removing about 30 CZs from D(10,5) (117 → 85) is worth roughly a third to a half more correct-weight shots. That fraction is exactly the f term that dominates the score at large C(N,n). The concrete construction to copy is a two-level split, done directly on the hardware patch. First, a fixed-weight divide block (it accepts only l = k, so it is cheaper than a general WDB) distributes the excitations across two adjacent rows or halves. Then the two halves are conquered in parallel as mirrored chains, and leaf orientation is chosen so that no reversal SWAPs are needed. That is Aktar's split on a ladder or B&E 2022's 31-CZ D(8,2) skeleton. Building D(6,3) in 23 CZ on a 2×3 ladder will settle whether the 5K(N−K)−3(N+K)+5 formula survives on grid connectivity. The asymptotic grid schemes (O(√(Nk)), O(k log(N/k)+n₂)) give little at N ≲ 20. Their depth floor is the patch side of 4–5, far below what the circuits actually need, and their scalable gadgets carry expensive Fredkin stairs. For N ≤ 6, a direct CZ-native search on the actual Willow subgraph is the right tool, as the team's D(4,2) = 5 CZ already shows. That result beats every published count, including Wang et al.'s all-to-all 6.

Three cross-cutting lessons from the literature support the team's strategy. Generic routing is expensive: Wang's dead-coupler collapse and IQM's 25% median routing overhead are the published evidence that building on a defect-free, low-error patch and relabelling qubits beats compiling a fixed circuit with SWAPs. **No Dicke paper does error-map-aware placement or permutation-symmetric relabelling, so this is open ground.** Readout asymmetry (|1⟩ errors about twice |0⟩ errors) supports the n ≤ N/2 form and placing likely-1 qubits on low-e₁ sites. Building from blocks that preserve the Hamming weight as a whole (Givens = 2 CZ, the controlled Givens/SWAP gadgets) means Z-type dephasing and CZ phase errors commute with the weight and cannot lower f. Only T1 decay, X/Y-type errors and readout errors can.

For the adaptive bonus, the literature rules out most of what it offers. Every constant-depth Dicke scheme except LAQCC is post-selected or approximate: Luo–Chen–Ma succeeds with probability ≥1/k, and Piroli–Styliaris–Cirac, the IBM protocol and Yu et al. all repeat until success. A circuit that is exact on every branch cannot be built from them. LAQCC's small-k Dicke route needs O(N² log N) qubits, about 225 or more at N = 15, which does not fit. Measurement-based fan-out costs about 5 CNOT layers, one measurement moment and one feedforward moment, roughly 12–13 Cirq moments. A unitary breadth-first fan-out over a 15-qubit grid patch of radius 3–5 costs about 8–10 moments, so measurement-based fan-out rarely pays off at N ≤ 15. The ideas worth borrowing are narrower. The first is LAQCC's "compress" step: a one-hot register can be disentangled from a binary index register by measuring the index in the X basis and applying single-qubit Z corrections, because the phase depends only on which qubit holds the 1. That is an exact, deterministic step costing two moments. The second is measurement-based uncomputation of AND-type ancillas. The third is teleportation-style long-range CNOTs, only where they replace a long sequential chain. The handoff notes currently cite arXiv:2406.07611 as a fan-out reference. It should be replaced with arXiv:2408.03064, since 2406.07611 is the Koh–Koh–Thompson readout-mitigation paper.

## Conclusion

The literature splits sharply. Theory has largely solved Dicke preparation asymptotically: depth-optimal on chains, near-optimal on grids, constant depth with feedforward or global gates. The engineering problem, as small a CZ count as possible on a real defective lattice with exact amplitudes, is barely studied. The only CZ-only grid experiment used a 31-gate circuit hand-tuned for a perfect patch and lost certified entanglement as soon as couplers died. For a team scored on hardware, the advantage comes from constant factors and placement, not from any asymptotic construction. The field's best small-case numbers come from exhaustive search and from the "only the reachable inputs matter" pruning trick, and both work better when run directly on the device graph than on an idealised one. The team's D(4,2) = 5 CZ already shows that.

One uncertainty matters most and is cheap to resolve. If Aktar et al.'s 5K(N−K)−3(N+K)+5 count survives on a 2-row ladder, it cuts roughly a quarter to a third of the CZs from every mid-sized instance in the current pipeline, since the ladder is a subgraph of Willow's square grid. If it needs all-to-all connectivity, B&E 2022's 31-CZ D(8,2) grid skeleton is the best structure available, and the team's peephole re-synthesis, which also exploits reachable inputs only, is the right tool to extend it to D(8,4) and D(10,5). For the bonus, the honest reading of the adaptive literature is that measurement helps exact Dicke circuits only in narrow places: disentangling index registers and shortcutting long chains. It is not a way to reach constant depth at N ≤ 15.
