# Dicke-state preparation by numerically optimised / variational circuits and by analog / native-interaction approaches

Scope note: the main deterministic Bärtschi–Eidenbenz (B&E) constructions, 2D-grid constructions and measurement-based methods are covered by other researchers and only mentioned in passing. Willow context for the reader: the hackathon only allows CZ + PhasedXZ (no iSWAP/fSim, no analog pulses), so the analog and native-gate material matters mostly as (i) evidence about how many two-qubit operations the state "really" needs, and (ii) design ideas such as keeping the state inside one Hamming-weight shell.

## Q1. Which approaches produce exact vs approximate Dicke states, and how does fidelity scale with N?

### Takeaway
Circuit-synthesis methods (exact CNOT search, AshN/SU(4) circuits, hand-built B&E-type circuits) give exact Dicke states when noiseless; analog methods (adiabatic passage, phonon-to-spin transfer, blockade, cavity-mediated interactions, ring frustration, optimal global control) are approximate and have only been shown with high fidelity for small N (about 2–11 qubits, measured fidelity about 0.77–0.93), apart from BEC experiments that make very large but non-addressable collective Dicke-like states. In every hardware report, measured fidelity drops steadily as N grows, and gate count/duration is the main cause.

### Cited Findings

**Exact numerical / compiler synthesis (gate-based, exact when noiseless)**
- Wang, Tan, Cong, De Micheli (DATE 2024, arXiv:2401.01009), "Quantum State Preparation Using an Exact CNOT Synthesis Formulation". Plain idea: treat state preparation as a shortest-path search. Each node is a quantum state, each edge is a gate (Ry costs 0, CNOT 1, controlled-Ry 2, multi-controlled Ry 2^n CNOTs), and the search runs backwards from the target to |0…0⟩. States that differ only by single-qubit gates or qubit relabelling are merged into one node, which keeps the graph small (for 4-qubit uniform states with cardinality 6, the graph has 8008 raw states but only 47 after merging). The circuits are exact and use real amplitudes. The authors call it the first design-automation method to beat hand designs, halving the best CNOT count for a Dicke state. They assume all-to-all connectivity (a symmetric coupling graph is needed for the relabelling equivalence). Exact synthesis only switches on for n ≤ 4 and m ≤ 16; bigger states are first split up by divide-and-conquer — [arXiv:2401.01009](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.01009)
- The same paper names the best manual design it compares against as Mukherjee et al., needing 5nk − 5k² − 2n CNOTs for D(n,k) — [arXiv:2401.01009](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.01009)
- Mukherjee et al. (arXiv:2007.01681), "On Actual Preparation of Dicke State on a Quantum Computer": a hand-built D(4,2) with 22 CNOTs, cut to 12 CNOTs in a reduced version that runs on IBM "ibmqx2" — [arXiv:2007.01681](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.01681)
- Aktar et al. (arXiv:2112.12435), divide-and-conquer Dicke preparation: recovers the best-known 2n − 3 CNOT count for W states (k = 1) — [arXiv:2112.12435](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.12435)

**Variational / RL / ansatz-based (usually approximate or parametrised)**
- Variational quantum eigensolver (VQE) portfolio paper (arXiv:2403.04296): two "universal" ansätze for Dicke states with tunable amplitudes, built from a staircase of controlled-Ry and CNOT gates adapted from W-state preparation. They are hand-designed, not found by a learning method — [arXiv:2403.04296](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.04296)
- Gard et al., npj Quantum Information 6, 10 (2020), "Efficient symmetry-preserving state preparation circuits for VQE". Plain idea: start from a computational-basis state with the right particle number, then apply layers of a 2-qubit "exchange" gate that only moves an excitation between two qubits (it acts only on span{|01⟩,|10⟩}). The particle number (= Hamming weight) is therefore preserved exactly by construction, and the circuit uses the minimum number of parameters needed to span the symmetry subspace. The exchange block is reported as 3 CNOTs + 2 rotations and needs only nearest-neighbour coupling — [APS March Meeting 2020 abstract](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/mar/2020/f07/13); [Economou KITP slides](https://online.kitp.ucsb.edu/online/frontiers-oc20/economou/pdf/Economou_Frontiers20Conf_KITP.pdf). (The 3-CNOT figure comes from a secondary summary; I did not check it against the paper.)
- RL for circuit discovery: a 2026 review describes PPO agents that compile state-preparation circuits using energy-based rewards, which worked well for spin-Hamiltonian ground states. It does not report Dicke-specific results — [arXiv:2601.18953](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18953v1)
- RL gate-sequence optimisation with general single-qubit unitaries reaches higher state-preparation fidelity than a hardware-efficient ansatz at equal CNOT count (generic states, not Dicke) — [arXiv:2511.08096](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08096)
- RobustState (arXiv:2311.16035): noise-aware variational training to boost state-preparation fidelity on hardware (generic; not checked for Dicke-specific numbers) — [arXiv:2311.16035](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.16035)
- Collision-model Dicke preparation dataset (Vu, Nguyen, Özdemir, Müstecaplıoğlu, Ozaydin, arXiv:2608.30176, 31 Aug 2026): records the best-found noiseless fidelity against circuit depth for 5–14 qubits over many excitation numbers, with measurements on the 54-qubit IQM Emerald superconducting chip. Recording a "best-found fidelity vs depth" frontier implies the protocol is approximate at finite depth. The abstract does not give gate types or numbers — [arXiv:2608.30176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30176)

**Analog / native-interaction approaches (approximate; small N)**
- Trapped ions, phonon-to-spin transfer: Hume, Chou, Rosenband, Wineland, PRA 80, 052302 (2009), arXiv:0909.0046. Plain idea: put the ion chain's shared motion into a Fock state with m phonons, then one global laser pulse turns each phonon into a spin flip, so m excitations end up spread symmetrically over all N ions (a Dicke state). Numerics show small errors for m = 1, 2. The experiment made a 2-ion Dicke state (m = 1) in ²⁵Mg⁺ with an ²⁷Al⁺ helper ion — [arXiv:0909.0046](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0909.0046); [NIST record](https://www.nist.gov/publications/preparation-dicke-states-ion-chain)
- Trapped ions, collective STIRAP (arXiv:0802.3538): global addressing with delayed, partly overlapping laser pulses (a many-particle version of stimulated Raman adiabatic passage) creates Dicke states of any size. The authors say it is immune to spontaneous emission and tolerant of moderate parameter errors — [arXiv:0802.3538](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0802.3538). A later paper notes that this kind of adiabatic Dicke scheme needs a phonon Fock state as its starting state — [arXiv:1208.3411](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1208.3411)
- Trapped ions, global Mølmer–Sørensen (MS): a 2026 constant-depth Dicke paper notes that many-qubit FAN-OUT gates can be done with global MS interactions, giving GHZ-like correlations without breaking them into pairwise gates. This is the main way ion "global gates" feed into Dicke circuits — [arXiv:2601.10693](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10693). Dissipative W-state preparation with MS plus sympathetic cooling also exists — [NIST PDF](https://tf.nist.gov/general/pdf/3144.pdf)
- Global transverse control of Ising-coupled qubits: Stojanović & Nauth, PRA 108, 012608 (2023), arXiv:2302.12483. Plain idea: with all-to-all Ising (ZZ) coupling and only global X/Y rotations, three instantaneous global pulses and two Ising-evolution periods take |000⟩ to D(3,2). The design works inside the 4-dimensional permutation-symmetric subspace, total time about 0.95ħ/J. The authors say it generalises to N ≥ 4 (D(4,2) shown) — [arXiv:2302.12483](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12483)
- Muratori, Stojanović, Cuestas, Calarco, Motzoi (arXiv:2512.19545, Dec 2025): optimal global control for W and Dicke states on rings with nearest-neighbour Ising coupling. It uses the ring's dihedral symmetry (a basis of O(2^N/N) states) and gives either NMR-style pulse sequences or shaped pulses under always-on coupling. The authors say it is robust to control errors and much faster than Rydberg coherence times; the abstract gives no fidelity numbers — [arXiv:2512.19545](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.19545)
- Rydberg blockade: theory shows adiabatic passage under blockade can make collective J_x = 0 eigenstates and GHZ states for tens of atoms — [PRL 100, 170504](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.100.170504). Experiment: chirped adiabatic rapid passage makes single collective Rydberg excitations (W-like "superatom" states) in mesoscopic ensembles and is more robust than resonant pulses — [arXiv:2101.01987](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.01987). Collective Rabi flopping in 3–16-atom ensembles shows the √N speed-up and gave N = 1 and N = 2 Fock-like collective states at fidelities 62% and 48% — [PRL 112, 043602](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.112.043602). Two tweezer atoms were excited into an entangled singly-excited (W₂) state — [arXiv:0810.2960](https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/nawi.inst.220/lehre/QIV_SS2008/0810.2960v1.pdf). A cavity plus blockade proposal synthesises collective states with π or STIRAP pulses — [arXiv:1504.04898](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.04898)
- Rydberg, W states by ring frustration: Catalano, Dağ, Torre, Giampaolo, Franchini (arXiv:2510.17974; v2 July 2026). An antiferromagnetic ring with an odd number of sites hosts one delocalised defect, which is a W state. Up to 11 Rydberg atoms, F ≈ 0.77, with a Bayesian tomography method for certification — [arXiv:2510.17974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.17974)
- Cavity / circuit QED: Wang, Scully, Agarwal (arXiv:2608.22168, Aug 2026). A dispersive cavity gives an effective collective-spin interaction. Add a coherent transverse drive, start in the drive's ground state, then slowly lower the drive while raising the cavity interaction ("adiabatic ground-state interpolation"). The detuning chooses which Dicke state |J, m⟩ you land in, including the m = 0 sensing state. Theory only, aimed at superconducting circuit QED, no fidelity numbers in the abstract — [arXiv:2608.22168](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22168). Ultrastrong-coupling circuit-QED proposal: nanosecond-scale Dicke-state generation via selective resonant interactions (theory) — [PRA 95, 013845](https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.95.013845)
- Superconducting, single-step analog W states: Romeiro, Roy, …, Filipp (arXiv:2605.18962, May 2026). Simultaneous engineered couplings spread one excitation over a lattice at once, so total time depends only on the largest lattice side. Results: 6-qubit W on a 3×2 lattice in 99 ns with tomographic fidelity 83.9 ± 1.0%; 7-qubit chain in 264 ns with 79.6 ± 1.3% — [arXiv:2605.18962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18962)
- BEC / spinor condensates (very large N, but collective and not individually addressable): driving a ⁸⁷Rb condensate through two quantum phase transitions gives a near-deterministic twin-Fock (spin-1 Dicke-like) state of about 11,000 atoms with phase sensitivity beyond the standard quantum limit — [arXiv:1702.03120](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.03120); an APS abstract describes about 11,700 atoms near |l = N, m = 0⟩ made by sweeping the quadratic Zeeman shift — [DAMOP 2018 abstract](https://https-meetings--archive-aps-org-443.webvpn1.xju.edu.cn/damop/2018/u08/1/); the theory proposal for adiabatic sweeps across the QPT — [arXiv:1308.2318](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1308.2318)

**Gate-based superconducting demonstrations with measured fidelities (exact circuits, noisy hardware)**
- AshN unified control (arXiv:2502.03612; Nature Physics 2025, s41567-025-02990-x): W₁₀ with 9 two-qubit gates, F = 0.913 ± 0.012; D(4,2) with 8 SU(4) gates, F = 0.926 ± 0.001; qubits Q0–Q9 on a 72-qubit transmon–coupler–transmon processor; average 2-qubit gate fidelity 99.37 ± 0.07% — [arXiv:2502.03612](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03612v1); [Nature Physics](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-025-02990-x)
- A follow-up (arXiv:2608.24084) makes D(8,2) on a 2×4 patch with AshN gates, F = 0.736 ± 0.001, compared against CZ compilation — [arXiv:2608.24084](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.24084) (CLAUDE.md records the CZ version as 31 CZ at F = 0.631)

### Inferences
- Plain summary on exactness: anything written as a fixed discrete circuit with analytically or numerically solved angles (exact synthesis, B&E, AshN circuits) is exact when noiseless. Adiabatic, STIRAP, blockade, cavity and optimal-control pulse schemes are approximate: errors come from finite ramp time, leakage outside the symmetric subspace, and atom-number uncertainty. Only the exact family passes the hackathon rule that circuits must be exact when noiseless.
- Fidelity vs N on real hardware, from the numbers above: about 0.93 (4 qubits) → 0.91 (W₁₀, gate count N − 1 only) → 0.84/0.80 (analog W, 6–7 qubits) → 0.74 (D(8,2), AshN) → 0.77 (11-atom Rydberg W). The W₁₀ result shows that a W state is cheap (gate count linear in N, small constant), while two-excitation states on a 2×4 patch already fall to about 0.7. This matches the scorer finding in CLAUDE.md that Hamming-weight retention and CZ count are the levers.
- Variational/RL methods in the literature add little for exact Dicke states. Their outputs are generally approximate, and the best exact small-instance results come from exhaustive or shortest-path search (Wang et al.), not gradient descent. Variational ideas are most useful here as a re-synthesis tool: optimise angles of a fixed CZ skeleton until infidelity is numerically zero, as the repo's template/peephole pipeline already does.

### Gaps
- I could not get gate types, fidelity tables or the parent protocol for the collision-model dataset (arXiv:2608.30176); the abstract page gives no numbers.
- No paper found that uses deep RL specifically to discover exact Dicke circuits. The searches returned only generic RL state-preparation papers.
- I did not fetch trapped-ion Dicke experiments beyond Hume 2009 (e.g., later multi-ion Dicke states with composite pulses or global MS). These are pointers to check, not verified.
- The Gärttner blockade-based W-state proposal (said to have fidelity independent of atom number) appeared only in a search summary; I did not retrieve or verify it.

## Q2. Best known minimal two-qubit-gate counts for small Dicke states from numerical search

### Takeaway
With CNOTs and all-to-all connectivity, the best published numerical counts are D(4,2) = 6, D(5,2) = 16, D(6,2) = 22, D(6,3) = 25 CNOTs (Wang et al. 2024; exact within their gate library, not proven globally optimal). For W_N the best known CNOT count is 2N − 3, with a proven lower bound of (15N − 3)/14 CNOTs and N − 1 if arbitrary SU(4) gates are allowed. The repo's D(4,2) = 5 CZ already beats the best published CNOT figure I found.

### Cited Findings

  - (4,1): 7 / 9 / 14 / 141 / **7**
  - (4,2): 12 / 24 / 14 / 141 / **6**
  - (5,1): 10 / 13 / 30 / 237 / **10**
  - (5,2): 20 / 67 / 30 / 601 / **16**
  - (6,1): 13 / 17 / 62 / 241 / **13**
  - (6,2): 28 / 117 / 62 / 645 / **22**
  - (6,3): 33 / 231 / 62 / 779 / **25**
  - Geometric mean 13.0 (manual) vs 10.9 (exact), a 17% improvement. Their D(4,2) circuit (Fig. 6) uses 6 CNOTs with Ry(π/4), Ry(π/2), Ry(0.39π) rotations and is described as "irregular", meaning not a generalisable pattern — [arXiv:2401.01009](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.01009)
- The same paper's sparse-state benchmark (m = n random sparse states) gives 3, 6, 9, 14, 20, 27, 37, 44 … 192 CNOTs for n = 3 … 20. This is a rough guide to what generic sparse-state methods cost (W/Dicke-like supports) — [arXiv:2401.01009](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.01009)
- Code is open source: github.com/Nozidoali/quantum-xyz — [arXiv:2401.01009](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.01009)
- W states: the AshN paper states N − 1 two-qubit gates is the lower bound for W_N without multi-qubit gates or ancillas, and hits it (9 gates for W₁₀). For CNOT-based W preparation it cites a provable lower bound of (15N − 3)/14 CNOTs, and says numerical evidence suggests the true CNOT minimum is 2N − 3 — [arXiv:2502.03612](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03612v1)
- 2n − 3 CNOTs for W states is "best-known" according to the divide-and-conquer paper — [arXiv:2112.12435](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.12435)
- Graph-theoretic lower bounds on CNOT circuits that make W-like states (limited entangling power of CNOT gives a "good graph" condition) — [arXiv:2105.06074](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.06074)
- Gate-count conflict for D(4,2): the AshN paper says "an existing exact-synthesis scheme" needs 14 CNOTs for D(4,2) — [arXiv:2502.03612](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03612v1). Wang et al. report 6 — [arXiv:2401.01009](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.01009). (14 matches the "n-flow" column in Wang's table, so the AshN paper probably compared against an older method.)
- With arbitrary SU(4) gates, D(4,2) was made with 8 SU(4) operations — [arXiv:2502.03612](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03612v1)
- Other all-to-all constructions: a 2022 short-depth paper reports a 27-CNOT circuit and improves on an earlier explicit 44-CNOT count. The snippet does not say which (n, k) these refer to — [arXiv:2207.09998](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.09998)

### Inferences
- Wang et al.'s numbers are optimal only within their library (Ry, CNOT, controlled-Ry, multi-controlled Ry) and under their search. They are not a proof of global minimality. Evidence: their W₄ = 7 and W₅ = 10 are worse than the known 2N − 3 = 5 and 7. So these figures are upper bounds, not floors.
- CZ and CNOT differ only by single-qubit gates, so the counts carry over directly to Willow's CZ + PhasedXZ gate set, before routing. The repo's results (CLAUDE.md: D(4,2) 5 CZ, W broadcast tree 2N − 3 CZ, D(6,3) 35 CZ on the hardware graph) can be checked against this: D(4,2) 5 < 6 published; W matches the best known; D(6,3) 35 vs 25 all-to-all suggests about 10 CZ of routing/connectivity overhead, or room for further search.
- A useful lower-bound sanity check: N − 1 two-qubit gates (any SU(4)) is necessary for W_N, and each CZ is a weak SU(4) gate. 2N − 3 CZ for W_N is therefore within a factor of 2 of the absolute floor and equals the conjectured CNOT optimum.

### Gaps
- No published exhaustive-search CZ/CNOT counts found for D(5,2) or D(6,3) restricted to nearest-neighbour (grid/path) connectivity, which is what matters on Willow.
- No proven lower bound on CNOT count for D(n,k) with k ≥ 2 was found.
- The (15N − 3)/14 bound and the "2N − 3 numerical evidence" are cited from the AshN supplement as reported in its main text; I did not read the supplement itself.

## Q3. How excitation-preserving native gates (iSWAP, fSim, XY/Givens) help keep the state in the right Hamming-weight shell, and which superconducting papers use this

### Takeaway
Gates that act only inside span{|01⟩, |10⟩} plus phases (XY/iSWAP family, Givens rotations, the particle-conserving "A" gate, fSim) can never change the number of 1s. A Dicke circuit built only from them, starting from a weight-k basis state, stays in the weight-k shell by construction. Gate errors then mostly mix amplitudes within the shell instead of leaking out of it. Superconducting demonstrations include the AshN W₁₀/D(4,2)/D(8,2) work, XY-gate calibration on Rigetti, and analog W-state spreading by simultaneous XY couplings. On Willow (CZ only) these gates must be compiled, and each excitation-preserving Givens rotation costs 2 CZ.

### Cited Findings
- Symmetry-preserving VQE circuits: start from a basis state with the right particle number and apply number-conserving exchange gates. They need only nearest-neighbour coupling and span exactly the symmetry subspace with a minimal number of parameters — [APS 2020 abstract](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/mar/2020/f07/13); [Economou KITP slides](https://online.kitp.ucsb.edu/online/frontiers-oc20/economou/pdf/Economou_Frontiers20Conf_KITP.pdf). Survey context on particle-conserving ansätze (Barkoutsos et al. PRA 2018 is the origin of the key gate) — [VQE review arXiv:2111.05176](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.05176)
- Rigetti: the whole XY(θ) family (excitation-preserving partial iSWAP) was implemented with one calibrated pulse (Abrams et al., arXiv:1912.04424, "Implementation of the XY interaction family with calibration of a single pulse") — [arXiv:1912.04424](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.04424)
- AshN gate (arXiv:2502.03612): a unified control scheme that combines the native iSWAP-type coupling with qubit driving to realise any SU(4) in one pulse. Used for W₁₀ (N − 1 = 9 gates, F = 0.913) and D(4,2) (8 SU(4), F = 0.926) — [arXiv:2502.03612](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03612v1). The follow-up D(8,2) on a 2×4 lattice (F = 0.736) frames AshN gates as a way around connectivity defects; a secondary summary claims about 45% fewer two-qubit gates on benchmarks (unverified) — [arXiv:2608.24084](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.24084); [qubitsok summary](https://qubitsok.com/papers/2608.24084)
- Analog W states from simultaneous engineered couplings on a superconducting 3×2 lattice and 7-qubit chain (one excitation spread in a single step; time set by the largest lattice side) — [arXiv:2605.18962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18962)
- Bus-resonator collective coupling: one-axis-twisting with up to 20 superconducting qubits on a common bus resonator, and 6 Xmons with virtual-photon exchange giving a collective transverse field plus a quadratic interaction. Both are reported as background in the collision-model dataset paper — [arXiv:2608.30176 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30176v1)
- The repo's own baseline notes (CLAUDE.md): a numerical search needed 2 CZ for the Givens 01 ↔ 10 gadget and found no 4-CZ version of the 5-CZ controlled Givens/SWAP gadget. This is consistent with the general fact that an excitation-preserving rotation needs 2 CZ (see Inferences).

### Inferences
- Why weight preservation helps scoring (plain words): the hackathon Z-score depends mostly on f, the fraction of shots with the right Hamming weight. With iSWAP/XY-native gates a coherent over- or under-rotation only redistributes amplitude among weight-k strings, so f is unaffected. With CZ + single-qubit gates, a mis-rotated single-qubit gate between CZs can create wrong-weight amplitude. On Willow we cannot use native iSWAP, but the design rule still applies: build the circuit from blocks that are excitation-preserving as whole blocks (Givens = 2 CZ, the 5-CZ controlled gadget). Then any Z-type dephasing or CZ phase error commutes with Hamming weight and cannot lower f. Only T1 decay, X/Y-type errors and readout errors do.
- Cost conversion: a real Givens rotation exp(θ(|01⟩⟨10| − |10⟩⟨01|)) has two nonzero Cartan (KAK) coordinates, so it needs at least 2 CZ/CNOT. An iSWAP-native chip does it in 1 native gate. Roughly, CZ-compiled Dicke circuits pay about twice the entangling-gate count of an XY-native implementation (compare W₁₀: 9 AshN vs 2N − 3 = 17 CZ). This is a property of the Willow gate set, not a defect in the team's circuits.
- Dephasing (Z) errors commute with the Hamming-weight operator, so in an excitation-preserving circuit they only damage the X-basis term. This matches the CLAUDE.md finding that the X term is small unless dephasing is large.

### Gaps
- I did not find a Google/Willow paper that prepares Dicke states with fSim/√iSWAP gates. Google's Givens-rotation Hartree–Fock and fSim work exists, but I did not verify any Dicke-specific use in this session.
- AshN circuit diagrams (exact decomposition of each SU(4), and whether they are XY-type) are in figures/supplement not captured by the text fetch.

## Q4. Dicke states as initial states for constrained QAOA with Hamming-weight-preserving (XY) mixers, and the preparation circuits used

### Takeaway
Constrained QAOA (portfolio optimisation, extractive summarisation, graph colouring, max-k vertex cover) uses XY mixers that conserve Hamming weight. The Dicke state, the uniform superposition over the feasible set, is the natural starting state because it is the ground state of the complete-graph XY mixer. Hardware runs reached 20–32 trapped-ion qubits. These papers treat Dicke preparation as a cost-heavy subroutine and normally use B&E-type circuits; the abstracts I could read do not give preparation gate counts.

### Cited Findings
- He, Shaydulin, Chakrabarti, Herman, Li, Sun, Pistoia, npj Quantum Information 9, 121 (2023), arXiv:2305.03857: QAOA works best when the initial state is the ground state of the mixer (the adiabatic picture). Shown numerically for constrained portfolio optimisation at p ≤ 3 and p = 100. XY-mixer QAOA was run on a 32-qubit trapped-ion processor — [arXiv:2305.03857](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03857); [npj QI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41534-023-00787-5)
- Same work (per search summary): uses a Dicke state with step-1 Trotterised ring-XY and complete-XY mixers. Complete-XY beats ring-XY under realistic noise for circuits under 200 two-qubit gates. On Quantinuum H2-1 the Dicke state served as a proxy for the hard-to-prepare ring-XY ground state, and extra alignment did not significantly help on hardware — [arXiv:2305.03857 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.03857)
- Niroula, Shaydulin, Yalovetzky, Minssen, Herman, Hu, Pistoia, Sci. Rep. 12, 17171 (2022), arXiv:2206.06290: XY-QAOA for extractive summarisation on a trapped-ion computer, up to 20 qubits and two-qubit depth up to 159. They show a trade-off between in-constraint probability and solution quality for unconstrained methods — [arXiv:2206.06290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.06290)
- The same paper cites Baker & Radha: up to 5 qubits with p = 5, complete-graph and ring XY mixers, Dicke or random in-constraint starting states, on Rigetti, IBM and IonQ hardware. QAOA beat random guessing for up to 3 qubits at p = 5 on Rigetti — [arXiv:2206.06290 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.06290)
- A review of near-term applications notes that the XY mixer needs an initial Dicke state, which adds significant overhead — [arXiv:2211.13080](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.13080)
- Graph colouring with XY mixers: the generalised W state performs better than easier classical starting states — [PRA 101, 012320 accepted manuscript](https://link.aps.org/accepted/10.1103/PhysRevA.101.012320) (this is Wang, Rubin, Dominy, Rieffel, "XY mixers: analytical and numerical results for QAOA"; I only saw it via the search summary)

### Inferences
- The QAOA literature adds no new preparation circuit. It consumes Dicke states and reports that preparation overhead and noise are the bottleneck, which is the same lever the hackathon scores.
- One transferable idea: preparing the ground state of an XY Hamiltonian adiabatically (start from a weight-k product state, ramp an XY coupling) is an approximate Dicke-like route. It is excluded here because the circuit must be exact.
